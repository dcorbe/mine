reclen 12, pagesize 512, DUP-permitting 4-byte key (dup512-style: same
geometry as tools/btrieve-oracle round-1 dup512 experiments).

1. create; insert 1..10 (unique baseline keys); insert four 3-member
   duplicate groups, keys 100/200/300/400, members tagged 1/2/3 in
   insertion order -> 00-baseline-all-groups.dat
2. delete_nth(100, n=0): deletes the HEAD (tag 1) of group 100
   -> 01-head-deleted-group100.dat
3. delete_nth(200, n=1): deletes the MIDDLE (tag 2) of group 200
   -> 02-middle-deleted-group200.dat
4. delete_nth(300, n=2): deletes the TAIL (tag 3) of group 300
   -> 03-tail-deleted-group300.dat
5. delete_nth(400, n=0), three times in a row: 3 members -> 2 -> solo (1)
   -> group eliminated (0)
   -> 04-group400-3-to-2.dat, 05-group400-2-to-1-solo.dat,
      06-group400-eliminated.dat

Findings: deleting the HEAD updates the leaf entry's own `head` field to
the second member's position, and that member's own `prev` link becomes
NOWHERE (it is the new head) -- `tail` and the entry count are untouched.
Deleting the TAIL is the mirror image (`tail` moves, the new-tail's `next`
becomes NOWHERE). Deleting a MIDDLE member changes neither `head` nor
`tail` -- only the two neighbouring records' `prev`/`next` links skip over
it, exactly a doubly-linked-list node removal. Draining a group down to a
SOLO member and deleting that one behaves exactly like deleting any
unique key's only record: the leaf's entry disappears entirely (entry
count drops by one), not a special "empty duplicate group" state.
